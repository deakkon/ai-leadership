# Site copy conventions

The main readers are B2B founders, product/technology owners and SME leadership teams. Some are technical; others are deciding where AI belongs before choosing tools or hiring engineers.

- Write as Jurica: use “I” for the practice and “we” when explicitly describing work agreed with the client. Identity labels and quotations keep their proper names and original voice.
- Start with the buyer's problem and decision. Explain technical terms when they affect that decision; retain necessary terms in detailed engineering and commercial descriptions.
- Use **service** for AI strategy, software development or workflow automation. Use **package** for the scope/fee model. Keep the four package names consistent.
- Say what an action opens: “Compare packages”, “See package details”, “Book an introductory call”, “Write a project brief”. Preparing a brief does not send it.
- Describe the first conversation without inventing its duration, response times, available dates or free/paid status.
- Keep prices, minimum terms, payment schedules, capacity, exclusions and acceptance terms explicit. Do not simplify away production-model costs, retries, human review, support or ownership responsibilities.
- Keep career evidence separate from agency engagements. Do not add employer/client names, results, company affiliations or testimonials without a source and permission. Illustrative examples remain labeled as such.
- Preserve the approved LinkedIn recommendation verbatim. Founder education and role facts come from the existing career evidence.
- Remove filler labels and repeated promises. A credential or paragraph must help the visitor decide; decorative initials are not a substitute for a real portrait.
- Keep legal/privacy drafting separate from marketing editing. Those pages require the confirmed operator and actual service-provider details; this content pass does not complete them.

The visual design remains light, uses one font family and retains the forest green/lime palette. The compact founder introduction uses existing education credentials; no portrait or substitute image has been generated.
